Lesson 5: Day-to-day transactions

  • Rent charges and collections, and tenant receivables
  • Payroll, maintenance costs, and utilities
  • Month-end close

Recording everyday transactions

Every entry follows the same rule: debits equal credits, and the entry is recorded in the program where the activity happened. The examples below use round numbers for illustration.

TransactionDebitCredit
Charge monthly rent of $450Tenant receivable $450Tenant rent revenue $450
Tenant pays the $450Cash $450Tenant receivable $450
Collect a $300 security depositCash $300Tenant security deposits (a liability) $300
Receive $10,000 of HUD operating subsidyCash $10,000HUD operating grant revenue $10,000
Buy $800 of maintenance supplies on accountMaintenance materials expense $800Accounts payable $800
Pay that $800 invoiceAccounts payable $800Cash $800
Run payroll: $2,000 gross pay, $300 withheldSalaries expense $2,000Cash $1,700; Payroll withholdings payable $300
Pay $5,000 of housing assistance to landlords (voucher program)HAP expense $5,000Cash $5,000

Two points trip up new staff. A security deposit is a liability, not revenue, because it belongs to the tenant until the lease ends. And payroll should be charged to the program or project where the employee worked, with shared staff time allocated using a documented method.

Tenant receivables

Keep an aging report showing who owes rent and how long it has been owed. Uncollectible balances are written off or covered by an allowance under the authority's policy, and write-offs should be approved and documented.

Month-end close checklist

  1. Reconcile every bank account to the books.
  2. Review the tenant receivable aging and record adjustments.
  3. Record accrued expenses, such as payroll and utilities not yet billed.
  4. Record depreciation on the schedule your policy requires.
  5. Tie out due to / due from balances between programs and the COCC.
  6. Compare actual results to the budget and explain large differences.
  7. Review the trial balance, then produce monthly reports for management and the board.

Practice: A tenant pays a $250 security deposit at move-in. Which accounts are debited and credited, and why is no revenue recorded?

Revenue recognition for HUD grants and other accounting policies can be technical. Confirm your approach with your auditor.
