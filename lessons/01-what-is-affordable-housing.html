Lesson 1: What is affordable housing?

Housing is generally called affordable when a household can pay for it without spending too much of its income on rent and utilities. Governments help by subsidizing either the family or the property. Each program below is funded, owned, and reported differently, which is why accounting varies by program.

ProgramHow the subsidy worksWho runs it
Housing Choice VoucherTenant-based: the subsidy follows the family to a private unitHousing authority
Mainstream VoucherTenant-based vouchers for non-elderly persons with disabilitiesHousing authority
Project Based Vouchers (PBV)Voucher assistance attached to specific unitsHousing authority
Project Based Rental Assistance (PBRA)HUD contracts with owners for assistance on specific unitsHUD, with private owners
New Construction / Substantial RehabilitationOlder project-based Section 8 contracts tied to specific propertiesHUD, with private owners
Public HousingHousing owned and operated by the authorityHousing authority
Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD)Converts public housing to long-term Section 8 contracts (PBV or PBRA)Housing authority, with partners
Multifamily HousingPrivately owned rental properties with HUD assistance or HUD-insured financingHUD Multifamily, with owners
Tax Credit (IRS)Low-Income Housing Tax Credit that funds construction or rehabilitation; not a HUD programIRS, administered by state agencies

Practice: A family receives a voucher and rents an apartment from a private landlord. Which program is this, and is the subsidy tied to the family or the unit?
