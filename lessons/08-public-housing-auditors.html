Lesson 8: Public housing auditors

  • What an independent auditor does and how the audit ties to the AFS and FDS
  • Independence: the auditor must be an independent external firm following Government Auditing Standards
  • Choosing an auditor and preparing for fieldwork
  • Common findings and how to avoid them

See the list of audit firms in the resources section.

What an auditor does

An independent public accountant (IPA) examines the authority's financial statements and gives an opinion on whether they are fairly presented. Audits of governmental entities follow Government Auditing Standards (the "Yellow Book"), and a Single Audit adds compliance testing when required. The auditor also checks that the FDS matches the audited financial statements and marks the audited FDS "IPA Agree" before the authority can submit it.

Independence

The auditor must be independent of the authority. An auditor should not make management decisions or review its own work. If the same firm helps prepare the statements, it needs safeguards under the standards, and staff must review and take responsibility for the result. A firm that also sells consulting, such as CBIZ, may run its audits through a separate CPA firm for this reason.

Choosing an auditor

  • Use a competitive procurement process that follows your written policy.
  • Ask about experience with housing authorities, HUD programs, and FDS submissions.
  • Check peer review results and ask for references from similar-sized authorities.
  • Confirm timing: the audited submission is due nine months after fiscal year end.

The audit timeline

  1. Engagement and planning meeting
  2. Interim testing of controls and transactions
  3. Year-end fieldwork after the books are closed
  4. Draft financial statements and findings, reviewed by management
  5. Auditor marks the audited FDS "IPA Agree"; the authority submits it to HUD
  6. Final report and Single Audit package filed with the Federal Audit Clearinghouse

How to prepare

  • Close the books and reconcile every account before fieldwork.
  • Have the trial balance, bank reconciliations, schedules, board minutes, and contracts ready.
  • Assign one staff member to coordinate the auditor's requests.
  • Fix last year's findings and keep proof that you did.

Practice: Why does the auditor need to approve the FDS before you submit the audited version?

Auditing and independence standards are technical and change over time. Ask your auditor about current requirements.
