Lesson 4: The Central Office Cost Center (COCC)

  • What the COCC is and why authorities use one
  • Where COCC revenue comes from: management, bookkeeping, and asset management fees
  • Typical COCC expenses and how costs are allocated
  • Transactions between the COCC and projects, including due to/from balances
  • How the COCC appears in the FDS and AFS

What the COCC is

The Central Office Cost Center (COCC) is the accounting unit for the authority's central overhead: the executive director, finance, human resources, IT, procurement, and board costs. Under HUD's asset management model, each public housing property is run and accounted for as its own project. Central costs are pulled out of the property books and recorded in the COCC, so each property shows what it really costs to operate.

Where COCC revenue comes from

  • Fees charged to projects and programs for central services, such as management, bookkeeping, and asset management fees on public housing.
  • Other allowable income the authority earns, such as fees for services to other programs.

What fees are allowed, and how much, is set by HUD's Operating Fund rules, so check current guidance before setting any fee.

Two ways to pay for central costs

MethodHow it worksResult
Fee for serviceThe COCC charges each project a fee for the services it providesProjects record a fee expense; the COCC records fee revenue
Cost allocationCentral costs are shared among projects and programs using a documented allocation planCosts are split by a reasonable basis, such as unit counts or staff time

A worked example

Maple Ridge Housing Authority (a made-up example) has two public housing projects with 100 units in total. It charges a management fee of $10 per unit per month, so the COCC earns $1,000 a month. The numbers are for illustration only.

WhereDebitCredit
Project booksManagement fee expense $1,000Due to COCC $1,000
COCC booksDue from projects $1,000Fee revenue $1,000
Later, when the project paysProject: Due to COCC $1,000; COCC: Cash $1,000Project: Cash $1,000; COCC: Due from projects $1,000

The project expense and COCC revenue are two sides of the same internal charge. When the authority's combined figures are prepared, the FDS has a place to eliminate these internal amounts so they are not counted twice, and the due to and due from balances between programs should offset each other.

Common mistakes and audit issues

  • Paying central office costs from a project's cash without recording a due to / due from balance.
  • Charging fees that are not supported by HUD rules or a written policy.
  • Allocating costs on a basis nobody can explain or document.
  • Leaving interprogram balances that do not offset at year end.

Practice: The central office pays a $600 IT invoice that serves the whole authority. Which accounting units should bear the cost, and what should be recorded between them?

Fee amounts, allowable costs, and reporting rules change. Confirm current requirements in HUD guidance and with your auditor.
