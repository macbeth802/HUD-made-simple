Lesson 7: Compliance and audits

  • Internal controls
  • Procurement basics
  • The Single Audit and common findings

Internal controls

Internal controls are the habits that keep mistakes and fraud from going unnoticed. Common ones include:

  • Separating duties: the person who approves a payment is not the person who prints the check or reconciles the bank account.
  • Approvals: written authorization for purchases, write-offs, and journal entries.
  • Reconciliations: bank accounts reconciled monthly and reviewed by someone else.
  • Access limits: passwords and permissions in the accounting system matched to job duties.

Procurement basics

Housing authorities buy goods and services with federal money, so purchases must follow federal procurement standards and the authority's own written procurement policy. In practice that means competition, documented price comparisons or bids, written contracts, and files that show why a vendor was chosen. Threshold amounts and methods are set in the policy and federal rules, so check the current ones.

The Single Audit

An authority that spends federal awards above a set annual threshold needs a Single Audit, which adds testing of compliance with federal program rules to the financial statement audit. The threshold is set in federal Uniform Guidance and has been raised over time, so confirm the current amount with your auditor. The audit reporting package, including the Data Collection Form, goes to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse within the earlier of 30 days after you receive the auditor's report or nine months after the end of the audit period.

Common findings

  • Capital Fund draws that are not supported by documentation
  • Weak cash management practices
  • Purchases that did not follow procurement policy
  • Late submission of required HUD forms and reports
  • Late submission of the audited financial statements and FDS, often because the books were not closed in time

Practice: An employee both approves vendor invoices and prints the checks. Which control is missing, and how would you fix it?

Thresholds, deadlines, and rules change. Confirm current requirements with HUD and your auditor.
