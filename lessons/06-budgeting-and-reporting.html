Lesson 6: Budgeting and reporting

  • Building an operating budget
  • Monthly and annual financial reports
  • Reporting to HUD and the board

The operating budget

The operating budget is the authority's plan for the year: how much money it expects to receive and how it will spend it. Under asset management, budgets are prepared for each project and program, plus the COCC, and the board approves them before the fiscal year begins.

  1. Start with last year's actual results and this year's trends.
  2. Estimate revenue: tenant rent, HUD operating subsidy, HCV funding from HUD's funding letter, and other income.
  3. Estimate expenses by category: administration, utilities, maintenance, insurance, and fees paid to the COCC.
  4. Check that each program's budget stands on its own and that restricted funds are used only for their purpose.
  5. Present the budget to the board for approval and keep the approved version on file.

Monthly budget-to-actual reports

Each month, compare actual results to the budget for every program. The variance is actual minus budget. Set a rule for which variances need an explanation, such as anything over a stated percentage or dollar amount, and give the board a short written explanation of the large ones.

Financial reports to HUD

  • Financial Data Schedule (FDS): submitted once a year, unaudited first and then audited (see Lesson 9).
  • Voucher Management System (VMS): monthly leasing and cost data for the voucher programs, which drives future funding.
  • PHA Plan and Capital Fund reports: the annual planning documents listed in Lesson 3.

Practice: Utilities are $25,000 over budget for the year. Whom should you tell, and what would you look for?

Budget requirements and reporting systems change. Confirm current HUD guidance and your authority's own policies.
